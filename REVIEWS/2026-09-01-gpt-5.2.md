### Findings

**src/tradebot/run.py — `run_once`**
1) **Claim:** `force=True` bypasses the global halt file and can trade after a kill switch  
   **Severity:** HIGH  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: `.halt` exists (e.g., drawdown previously triggered), operator runs `run_once(cfg, broker, force=True)`.  
   - Wrong behavior: The code checks `risk.halted()` *before* considering `force`, so you might think you’re safe—but `force` still bypasses the *idempotency* and *market_open* checks. If `.halt` is deleted accidentally (or temporarily unreadable) but state still indicates a kill condition, `force=True` will happily proceed to generate/submit orders the same day and/or while market is closed. This is a practical bypass path for “no trading after halt intent” because halt persistence is only the file; there is no secondary “halted in state” check for the slow arm.

2) **Claim:** Drawdown flatten orders are not risk-capped; can exceed `max_order_notional` and violate order-level caps exactly when kill-switch fires  
   **Severity:** HIGH  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: positions contain `{"SPY": 80.0}` market value (possible if price gaps up or if broker reports market_value > book_cap due to drift), `risk.max_order_notional=30`. Drawdown triggers.  
   - Wrong behavior: `flatten = [{"notional": abs(mv), ...}]` then `broker.submit(o)` directly; no call to `risk.filter_orders`. A single flatten order can be $80 notional, violating the explicit “no single order above $30” cap. This is exactly the state you most need hard brakes to be consistent.

3) **Claim:** `max_positions` enforcement is incorrect: it only limits count of **buy orders**, not resulting positions; can end the run with >`max_positions` holdings  
   **Severity:** HIGH  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: current positions already 2 names: `{"SPY": 25, "QQQ": 25}`; targets add a third and keep the first two (e.g., due to `diff_orders` bug elsewhere or manual position existing): targets `{"SPY": 20, "QQQ": 20, "IWM": 10}`.  
   - Orders: sells for SPY/QQQ deltas and a buy for IWM. `filter_orders` counts buys (`len(buys)==1`) so it approves.  
   - Wrong behavior: you now hold 3 positions (SPY, QQQ, IWM) even though `risk.max_positions=2`. The check needs to consider *final portfolio cardinality*, not number of buy tickets.

4) **Claim:** Idempotency is based on UTC date, not market session date; can run twice in one US trading day and place duplicate trades  
   **Severity:** MED  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: scheduler runs at 23:30 ET and again at 00:30 ET (which is next day in UTC). `today = datetime.now(timezone.utc).date()` changes, while the US market session hasn’t.  
   - Wrong behavior: `ledger.last_run_date()` is compared to UTC day string; the second invocation is not considered “already ran today” and may rebalance again off the same closes/positions, doubling turnover and potentially breaking `max_order_notional` via repeated partial rebalances.

**src/tradebot/risk.py — `RiskManager.book_equity`**
5) **Claim:** “Reset” re-anchoring logic can erase real losses and prevent drawdown kill switch from triggering (book-cap rebaseline bypass)  
   **Severity:** HIGH  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Config: `book_cap=50`.  
   - State: baseline previously 100000 (slow arm on the legacy $100k paper account per config comment).  
   - Event: large trading loss or other equity drop brings `raw` down to, say, `raw=90000`. That’s not `< baseline*0.5`, so no reanchor. Fine.  
   - But if the account is *manually* adjusted or the broker reports a temporarily low equity (API glitch, partial reporting, or paper “reset”) to `raw=80`, condition `(raw < baseline*0.5 and raw <= cap*2)` becomes true (`80 < 50000` and `80 <= 100`). Baseline becomes `80`.  
   - Wrong behavior: book equity becomes `min(50 + (raw-baseline), raw)=min(50+(80-80),80)=50` immediately, and the high-water mark logic in `check_drawdown` now compares against an equity series that has effectively forgotten the prior peak. A real loss path can be converted into an apparent reset and never trip the slow-arm drawdown halt, depending on the timing of the low-equity blip.

6) **Claim:** State read is not fail-closed; JSON corruption makes the bot crash instead of halting  
   **Severity:** MED  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: `state.json` exists but contains truncated/invalid JSON due to disk full/manual edit (`{"high_water_mark": 52` missing brace).

It fails open in the sense that the halt file isn’t written and no flatten occurs; depending on the outer runner/supervisor, it may be restarted repeatedly until the file is fixed, skipping kill-switch enforcement entirely during that period.

**src/tradebot/fastarm.py — `run_fast_once`**
7) **Claim:** EOD/kill-switch/daily-stop exits assume `side="sell"` always; shorts (or negative qty) are mishandled, leaving exposure after supposed flatten  
   **Severity:** HIGH  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: broker account holds a short from any cause (manual, borrow-enabled paper, or a bug elsewhere): `positions_detail()` returns `{"AAPL": {"qty": -1, ...}}`.  
   - Wrong behavior: on drawdown kill / daily loss stop / eod flat / overnight flatten it calls `_fill(..., "sell", ..., qty=-1, ...)`.  
     - In live mode, `_fill` maps `"sell"` to `broker.close(sym)` which is fine for liquidation *if the broker supports it*.  
     - In simulated mode (or if broker.close fails), it uses `st["cash"] += qty * fill_px` with negative qty, *reducing* cash and removing the position, and P&L math uses `pnl = (fill_px - entry_px) * qty` with negative qty, flipping sign. Net result: “flatten” can worsen equity or miscompute P&L, and the arm may not actually neutralize risk correctly in sim paths.

8) **Claim:** Look-ahead/stale-data bug: uses the last close of `intraday_5min(universe)` without bounding to `now`; can trade on bars after decision time  
   **Severity:** HIGH  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: `run_fast_once` called at 10:05 ET. `broker.intraday_5min(universe)` returns bars through 10:30 (common with vendor caching/default end time = 16:00 request; you do request start=9:30 end=16:00 in AlpacaBroker.intraday_5min).  
   - Wrong behavior: `lasts = df["c"].iloc[-1]` becomes a future price relative to `now`. `opening_range(df, or_end)` also uses `bars["t"] < or_end`, but if the dataframe includes later bars, the OR computation is fine; the *breakout check* uses `lasts[sym] > hi` with a future last, entering trades earlier than possible (pure look-ahead). This is a concrete path because the data request in `intraday_5min` explicitly asks through market close, not through `now`.

9) **Claim:** In live/broker mode, `trades_today` increments even if the broker order partially fills or stays pending; trade-count and risk throttles can be bypassed or misapplied  
   **Severity:** MED  
   **Failure scenario:**  
   - Inputs/state: broker.submit returns status `"accepted"`/`"new"` (not rejected/canceled), but the order doesn’t fill yet (or fills later).  
   - Wrong behavior: `_fill` returns True and `st["trades_today"] += 1`, consuming one of `max_trades_per_day` even though position may not exist. Conversely, later ticks may place additional buys because `_positions` (from broker) doesn’t show the pending fill yet, letting you submit more than intended and exceed `top_k` or buying power.
